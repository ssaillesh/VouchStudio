    How it works

    What the fit score means

    Each job's requirements are read from its description and weighted 3 for must-haves, 2 for required and 1 for nice-to-haves. The score is the share of that weight your profile backs with evidence. It measures coverage of what the posting asks for, not your chance of being hired.

    Master profile versus plain resume

    A master profile tags each line as verified or [NO], so skills you have ruled out never count. A plain resume has no tags: every line counts as your own claim, which makes the score less strict. The profile card shows which mode was used.

    Why some jobs say "not enough detail to score"

    A score needs at least three skill requirements the parser recognizes. Many sales, finance and operations postings name only a degree or terms outside the skill vocabulary, and a score built on one match would be misleading, so those jobs are listed without one.

    Why "Best match" can differ from the score

    Some postings yield only one or two recognizable requirements, so a 100 can rest on very little. Best match discounts scores built on few requirements; the score itself is shown unchanged on every job.

    Where your resume goes

    It is sent to this app's server on your own computer, parsed in memory for a single request, and never saved there. With "Remember on this device" checked, a copy stays in this browser's local storage so the board can re-score after a reload. "Forget my resume" removes it.
